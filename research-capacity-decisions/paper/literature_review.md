# Verified Literature Review

## 1. Forecast accuracy is not the same as operational value
Danese and Kalchschmidt, *International Journal of Production Economics* (2011), study 343 manufacturing firms and explicitly distinguish forecast accuracy from operational performance. Their conclusion is that forecasting processes can improve operational performance through mechanisms beyond accuracy alone. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijpe.2010.09.006.

## 2. Capacity decisions should be evaluated directly
Sagaert et al., *International Journal of Production Economics* 209 (2019), pp. 12–19, examine tactical capacity planning and state that forecast-accuracy measures are proxies for decision-associated costs. They evaluate forecasts through capacity/inventory simulation and argue that direct decision impact is preferable to relying only on forecast accuracy. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijpe.2018.06.016.

## 3. Recent evidence confirms the forecast-utility issue in inventory
Theodorou, Spiliotis and Assimakopoulos, *European Journal of Operational Research* 322(2) (2025), pp. 414–426, empirically examine forecast accuracy against inventory costs using M5 data. They find that the most accurate forecast is not always the preferable method when operational cost asymmetry changes. DOI: 10.1016/j.ejor.2024.12.033.

## 4. Recent ML work is increasingly integrating forecasts with operational decisions
Paull, Bubak and Stuckenschmidt, *Expert Systems with Applications* 271 (2025), Article 126586, combine ML-based probabilistic demand forecasting with stochastic master production scheduling under capacity constraints. DOI: 10.1016/j.eswa.2025.126586.

## 5. Dataset source
The NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission publishes yellow taxi trip records monthly in Parquet format. The records contain pickup/drop-off timestamps and locations, among other fields. The official TLC data page is the source of record for the dataset and download links.

## Research-positioning conclusion
The central idea of this project is therefore **not** that the literature has ignored forecast utility. It has not. The defensible contribution is narrower: an empirical, reproducible test of forecast-to-capacity alignment in an urban taxi service setting using public high-frequency demand data, simple interpretable ML models, and an explicit asymmetric capacity-cost proxy.

We will not claim “first study,” “no prior work,” or similar absolute novelty unless a comprehensive literature search establishes it.
